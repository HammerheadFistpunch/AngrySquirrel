"What in the world are you talking about?" 
You may be saying to yourself. 
"What's a Farkle-Meister? On that note, what's a Farkle?"

Look, there are a lot of ways to enjoy cars; You got your lowriders, your hot-rodders, Drifters, audiophiles, track stars, off-roaders, etc. We [Humans] Love to group things! Even the cars themselves are grouped; Wagons, sedans, hatchback, fastback, crossover, vans, etc. Car enthusiasts, like any passionate group, knows that it goes way beyond the broad strokes and each group has its own little subgroups to argue about and nitpick. The reality is that while we LIKE grouping, grouping isn't actually all that informative. There are, afterall, exceptions to every rule and C-C-Combo breakers that defy the norms.  

That being said, I want to drill down into my little niche of the car world, a sub group of off-roaders which includes crawlers, buggies, car campers, rally, and of course the hyper popular overlanders.

I've never really liked the term "overland" or "overlander" specifically because I think that it has a narrow definition that most overlanders don't actually meet. The term coming from the long distance cross-country travel that serves a practical purpose - pioneers, safari, prospecting, remote exploration (true exploration) and frankly isn't confined to, or even largely defined in the automotive space. Usually its on foot, on horse, or in industrial efforts with long timelines.  

I digress. The market has spoken and it doesn't really care for what I think of the term, the vehicle based adventure travel phenomenon has taken root in the lexicon as overlanding. But like all little niches, there is more than one type in the group and I think I've come up with a fun new set to argue about and invite you to join in.

To begin, lets start with a graph.

![The Cost of Ease](https://assets.tina.io/bce22d76-7099-40c4-9574-e69b5555a914/overlanding-cost-of-ease-(1).png)
labeled here as 1. Premium, 2. Minimalist, 3. Essentials and 4. Quantity

So what do we have here. On the left, is the total spending in dollabucks for a given overlander-sickness meds.  On the bottom from left to right, is how easy you make your life on the trail and at camp. Both in terms of time, effort, reliability and comfort.  

Basically as the line goes right, you have an easier and more enjoyable time on the trail and camp. As you go up, the more you spent to get there.

And so, my list. 

- 1. The Autobiography (Premium)

This is the person for whom money is largely a secondary concern as it relates to their hobbies.  They aren't necessarily foolish with their money (that's another category) but they don't mind paying a premium price for premium gear and experiences. They understand that you get what you pay for and they appreciate having nice, new things and the value that brings. These are the people that have very well sorted equipment and have people gather around their rigs at camp to see all the cool stuff. They probably have some kind of coffee machine or other bit of unnecessary gear because, hey, why not?! In the overland world, money buys time, effort and ease.  There are, however, diminishing returns at the top of this scale and nice stuff costs disproportionally more than slightly less nice stuff.  Still, if you've got it and you want to spend it so you can live free and happy on the trail. I just have one thing to say - we hate you.  Not really, but we are all secretly jealous. The flipside of that coin is that this person is less likely to have worked to earn the skills that come from the struggle on the lower part of the overland totem pole and they may not have a clear idea of what to do if something breaks. Some build their own gear and really like to get into the weeds, but in many cases a build is done at a shop. While this again saves time and effort, the costs are a lack of skills based knowledge that could make a meaningful distinction if something does go wrong.  

- 2. The Ghost (Minimalist)

This is one of my personal favorites. This person is very much like the The Autobiography in that they know good gear and don't mind spending money to get it, but through time, experience or simply preference have learned what they actually need to enhance their experience and what they can live without. The ease is maximized, the clutter minimized. This is the person who rolls into camp, pull out a chair and a drink and sits and watches everyone build their little villages. His campsite is already setup or requires such little effort that it doesn't make any sense to even start yet. The Ghost leaves a very light footprint and is generally courteous to their neighbors to the point that louder groups might not even know they are there. Every piece of gear has at least one purpose and most have multiple. Sure they often do without some of the creature comforts, but they are no less happy for it.  

- 3. The Strider (Essentials)

This could also be called the weekend warrior, only it doesn't need to apply only to those that can't get away as often as they'd like.  This is the person who understands what is required to do the job, understands the value of good parts, and prioritizes based on either cost or weight budgets.  If you don't NEED a steel bumper, don't buy one, and spend the money on nicer shocks instead or a fridge or something else that enhances your trip. If you spend your money making sure that your vehicle is in good shape and fully armored, then the other stuff like racks and tents might be a lesser priority.  Most times this is a budget constraint, but many in this group would rather just not make life any more complicated than it needs to be.  When The Strider rolls into town to top off with gas or get food, you may not even realize they are on day 3 of a 5 day adventure. When they get home, wash their rig and unpack they could daily it and be accused of being a mall crawler or simply ignored entirely. Stealth is a major beneficial byproduct of this type. 

- 4. The Farkle Meister (quantity)

Farkle is the overland term for all the crap that gets bolted to a car, sometimes for function but often for show.  The Farkle Meister is all about that overland style, at the expense of all else.  35 inch tires rub? Who cares! Got that stance yo!  Never drive off-road in the dark?  Pfft, gotta have ditch lights still. Winch? You know it! Even if there is nothing to winch off when you go out. You see The Autobiography and while most of us just drool you get to work: Amazon, harbor-freight, TEMU...you aren't fooled by those insane markups for name brands! When you finally get camp setup it looks like a living room threw up on the ground. String lights, hammocks, couches, bbq, etc.  Sure it takes an hour and 200 steps, but that's the price of comfort! Where the Ghost and The Autobiography know they are going to spend going in, The Farkle Meister hits the ground running with minimal cash outlay for maximum stuff, only to find out as they continue what "buy once, cry once" actually means. You are constantly replacing, and swapping out gear, your parts aren't holding up to any kind of abuse and you find that maybe you should have set a little more aside for preventative maintenance as broken parts start adding up. You'll either get sick of always working on your stuff and sell it at a loss, or start to migrate towards one of the other styles, but only after you've outspent what you thought you would.
